# Danish en or et: how to pick the right article

Is it en eller et? This is one of the first walls learners hit. It does not help that the
word itself rarely tells you. The good news is that you can guess better than you think. There
are patterns. You just need to know them.

## Why it matters

Gender spreads. It drives the article, the ending, and the word you reach for later in the
sentence. You say en bil, bilen, den er ny (a car, the car, it is new). But you say et hus,
huset, det er nyt. Pick wrong and a listener hears it four times in one sentence. That is why
a small slip feels bigger than it is.

## Start from the last part

The most useful trick is short. A long word takes its gender from its final part. En dør (a
door) gives en hoveddør. Et bord (a table) gives et skrivebord. So you do not need to learn the
long word. You only need the short one.

## Learn the smaller group

Most Danish nouns are common gender. So guess en when you know nothing. But guess with your
head. Learn the words that take et instead. That list is shorter. And it grows slowly.

## How to practise

Never learn a noun alone. Learn it with its article. Say et hus, not hus. Write down the words
you get wrong, and read the list aloud once a week. It is dull. It works.

## Questions people ask

**Can I hear the gender in the word?** Almost never. You have to learn it.

**What do I do when I am unsure?** Pick en. You will be right more often.

**Does the plural matter?** No. In the plural the difference drops away, and the choice is gone.
